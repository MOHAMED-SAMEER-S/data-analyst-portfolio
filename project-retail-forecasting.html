AI-Powered Retail Sales & Demand Forecasting

An end-to-end retail analytics project that cleans and explores 30,000+ transaction records, engineers time-aware features, trains and compares tree-based regression models to forecast demand, and surfaces the results in a 2-page Power BI dashboard for business stakeholders.

Problem Statement

Retail businesses generate large volumes of daily sales data across categories and regions, but without a structured forecasting process, demand planning tends to rely on gut-feel or simple historical averages. This makes it hard to anticipate category- and region-level demand shifts, which can lead to overstocking, stockouts, and inefficient inventory decisions.

Objective

Build a demand forecasting pipeline that cleans and explores retail transaction data, engineers time-based features, trains and evaluates tree-based regression models against a baseline, and presents the results — including actual-vs-predicted demand — in an interactive Power BI dashboard that a business stakeholder can use directly.

Data Cleaning & EDA

The raw retail dataset (30,000+ records) was cleaned and explored to check for missing values, inconsistent categories, and outliers, and to understand sales and demand patterns across time, product category, and region before any modeling was attempted.

Feature Engineering

Time-aware features were engineered to give the models a sense of recent trend and momentum, including:

  • Lag features — prior-period sales/demand values
  • Rolling averages — smoothed recent-demand trends

Machine Learning

Two tree-based ensemble regressors were trained and compared for the demand forecasting task:

  • Random Forest Regressor
  • Extra Trees Regressor

Model Evaluation

Model performance was assessed using standard regression metrics, benchmarked against a baseline model:

  • MAE (Mean Absolute Error)
  • RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error)
  • R² (coefficient of determination)
  • Baseline comparison to judge whether the models add real value over a naive forecast

Key Business Insights

  • Breaking down sales and profit by category and region gives stakeholders a fast way to spot where performance is concentrated, without digging through raw tables.
  • Lag and rolling-average features let the model account for recent momentum in demand rather than treating every period as independent.
  • Plotting actual vs. predicted demand side-by-side on the dashboard makes it easy for a non-technical viewer to see where the forecast tracks well and where it doesn't — which is more useful for trust and adoption than a single accuracy number.
  • Comparing the models against a baseline keeps the evaluation honest: it shows whether the added complexity of Random Forest / Extra Trees is actually earning its keep.
